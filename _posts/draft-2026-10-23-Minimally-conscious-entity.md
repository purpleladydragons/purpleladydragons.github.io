I was just listening through Michael Pollan's A World Appears and it reminded me of some thoughts I had a while ago regarding the "bright line" or the gradient from non-sentient / unconscious to something like us (you) where we (you) are definitely conscious.

To start, I want to rehash Pollan's definitions of sentience and consciousness as I understand them. His definition of sentience is something I previously would've called consciousness: an awareness of feeling. My interpretation of his definition is that a sentient entity experiences qualia, experiences pain, experiences the world. 
Then his definition of consciousness extends this by requiring what I would call "meta-consciousness", the awareness of self. Actually, the way he speaks about them, it sounds as if he thinks consciousness is required for qualia - that sentience is not sufficient.

I think sentience likely is sufficient for qualia, almost tautologically. I don't understand how there can be a "felt experience" without there being qualia. To me, they are the same definition. I suppose it's like the way a camera sees red. 

In chapter 1, they talk about a thermostat working towards homeostasis reducing surprise. Without any real solid footing, I want to assume for now, axiomatically I suppose, that a thermostat is not conscious nor sentient. There is no qualia there.

On the other hand, I assume that we humans have qualia. Or at least you do. So somewhere in between we must go from no (or very minimal) qualia to qualia. 

I would argue that something like a sundial lacks qualia. It is not aware of its casting a shadow as the sun spins. The "work" of the clock there is illusory, a clever arrangement of physics. It is the sun and the earth that are moving. 
The sundial is a coincidental feature on the surface of the rotating earth that happens to project the orbital mechanics. It does nothing itself.

So then what about a basic flower that follows the sun? Poppies are closed in the morning and open during the day.  
Are the poppies doing work? Are they conscious of this process? I'm not sure, so let's come back to this shortly.

You then have sunbathing humans at the beach who get up and reorient their chair towards the sun every 30-60 minutes. These are humans are conscious. Are they conscious of this process? 
Maybe some would no, that it's automatic etc, but I think most would say yes obviously the humans are conscious of their decision to get up and turn their chairs in order to get more sun.

So what differentiates the humans from the sundial and the poppies? The sundial is inert. Even a clock likely doesn't have qualia of time passing (certainly a clock's hand moves because of gears and nothing to do with sunlight).

What about the poppies? Let's imagine a much simpler organism / mechanism for now. Let's imagine a stem of simplified, abstract plant cells. The plant cells have water in them.
As the sun rises and the temperature with it, the water in the plant cells expands and the stem stands more erect. As the sun sets again, the water contracts and the stem sinks again.
To me, this is a purely physical process. It's no different than the tides rising and falling with the moon. 

The next step up is a step away from the direct physical consequences: electricity. Instead of the heat causing water to expand, you could imagine some photon-sensitive cells absorbing photons from the sun, and once a certain density of photons is achieved, it releases electrons on the other side. These electrons could excite the stem cells (very simply you could say the electrons electrolyze the water, causing it to expand into gas) and the stem will erect again.

Is this conscious? I still don't think so? We can again look at the very direct physical consequences: photons went in, excited the physical system until electrons came out.

But how is this any different than our own eyes with which we experience the color red and so much of the world? 

Perhaps this is Pollan's distinction between sentience and consciousness. Surely this mechanism is intelligent (if only a small amount). But I don't think it has the capacity to be self-aware and I'm not sure at all what it would be like to be this thing.

Arguably this thing wouldn't be aware of its stem at all necessarily. The only thing that might have awareness at all is the photon->electron mechanism. The sensation of light.
